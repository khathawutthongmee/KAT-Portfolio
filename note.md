## 1. Build a Consistent Visual Identity
To stand out as a mobile photographer, you need a cohesive "look" that makes your images recognizable.

* Pick a Niche: Focus intensely on one or two genres that suit the iPhone's strengths, such as street photography, minimalist architecture, documentary, or travel portraits. 
* Define Your Color Palette: Use mobile editing suites like Adobe Lightroom Mobile to create or purchase custom presets. Stick to consistent tones (e.g., warm cinematic tones, moody desaturated greens, or punchy high-contrast black-and-white). 
* Master the 24mm and 52mm Focal Lengths: Learn exactly how the world looks through your phone's native 1x (24mm wide-angle) and sensor-cropped 2x (52mm portrait-length) views without looking at the screen. 
------------------------------
## 2. Learn to "See" and Control Light
Great photography is entirely dependent on the quality of light, not the size of your camera sensor.

* Chase the Golden Hour: Shoot during the first hour after sunrise and the last hour before sunset. The low angle of the sun creates long, dramatic shadows and a soft, warm glow that makes mobile images look cinematic. 
* Embrace Harsh Midday Shadows: Use bright, harsh sunlight to your advantage by creating stark, graphic compositions. Expose for the brightest highlights so your shadows drop into deep, clean blacks.
* Utilize Physical Geometry: Train your eyes to look for leading lines (like tram tracks in Oostende or long pier railings in Nieuwpoort), natural frames (windows, archways), and repeating patterns. 

------------------------------
## 3. Move from Auto to Manual Control 
The default iPhone camera app wants to make every image bright and evenly lit. To create professional, artistic photos, you must take creative control.

* Tap and Slide to Underexpose: Always tap your subject on the screen, then slide the sun icon downward. Intentionally underexposing your shots preserves detail in the sky and gives your images a richer, less "smartphone-like" feel. 
* Use Third-Party Camera Apps: Download apps like Halide or ProCamera. These tools allow you to manually adjust your shutter speed, ISO, and white balance, giving you the same creative choices a DSLR photographer has. 
* Shoot in RAW Format: If using third-party apps, shoot in RAW or ProRAW format. This saves the uncompressed data from your 48MP sensor, giving you maximum flexibility to recover details in highlights and shadows during editing.  

------------------------------
## 4. Treat Mobile Photography as a Business
If your goal is to turn this into a career or a serious freelance pursuit, you need to showcase your work professionally.

* Build a Portfolio Website: Do not rely solely on social media. Use platforms like Squarespace, Format, or Pixpa to build a clean, high-resolution digital portfolio categorized by project or aesthetic.
* Publish Photo Essays: Instead of posting single, random photos, curate collections of 5 to 10 images that tell a cohesive story about a specific place, event, or person.
* Engage with the Community: Share your work on platforms dedicated to high-end mobile artistry. Use tags and participate in communities surrounding publications like Amateur Photographer or mobile-focused design galleries.

## Essential Shooting Tactics

* The Stealth Approach: Because your iPhone is small and ubiquitous, it rarely draws the attention or security scrutiny that larger, professional mirrorless cameras do. You can compose without bringing the phone to your eye, or use your Apple Earbuds to click photos remotely using the volume buttons. [1, 2, 3] 
* Embrace Bad Weather: Rainy days are perfect for street photography. Shoot into puddles to capture striking, inverted reflections of the city. [4, 5] 
* Contrast and Silhouettes: Look for harsh sunlight or deep shadows. Position bright subjects against dark backgrounds and use the built-in Noir filter to create dramatic, high-contrast black-and-white art. [5, 6] 
* Long Exposures: Turn on Live Photo in your camera app. After taking a photo of moving traffic or a busy pedestrian area, swipe up on the image and change it to Long Exposure to get smooth, professional motion blur. [7] 

## Key Camera Settings

* AE/AF Lock: Tap and hold on the screen to lock focus and exposure. This prevents your camera from hunting for focus when someone walks into your shot. [5] 
* Manual Exposure Adjustment: After tapping to focus, slide the little sun icon up or down to adjust your brightness. Underexposing by 1 or 2 stops can make your colors pop and keep bright highlights from blowing out. [5] 

## Exploring Local Spots
To practice around the Belgian coast, iconic nearby settings offer incredible subjects:

* The Zeedijk: The bustling promenades in Nieuwpoort-Bad offer excellent opportunities to capture candid moments of locals and tourists against the North Sea.
* Nieuwpoort Marina: The combination of modern yachts, historical boats, and geometric docks provides endless leading lines and symmetrical framing.
* Oostende: Just a short drive or coastal tram ride up the N34, the city center and the Mercator marina provide dense, urban environments perfect for street scenes.
